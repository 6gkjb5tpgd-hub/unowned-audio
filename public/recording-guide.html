Recording guide

You do not need a studio. You need a phone and a quiet-ish room. Done and a bit rough beats perfect and never recorded.

Before you start

  • Claim one chapter. Not three. You can always claim another after.
  • Read it through once first, out loud if you can. You’ll find the weird names and the sentences that run on forever before they ambush you mid-take.
  • Look up any names or words you can’t say. Forvo.com has people saying them. If you’re still unsure, pick one way and be consistent.
  • Check for content notes. If the chapter has violence, abuse, slurs in quotes or anything similar, note it so we can put it in the description.

Where to record

  • Somewhere small and soft. A wardrobe full of clothes is the classic for a reason. A duvet over your head works too. Don’t be shy about it, nobody can see you.
  • Turn off fridges, fans, notifications and the phone you’re not recording on.
  • Windows closed. Pets are lovely but they’ll sing along.
  • Avoid big empty rooms with bare walls. You’ll sound like you’re reading in a bathroom.

What to record with

  • Your phone is fine. Use the voice memo app. Hold it about a hand-width from your mouth, a bit to the side so your breath doesn’t thump the mic.
  • A headset or cheap USB mic is better if you have one. Don’t buy anything.
  • Keep the same distance the whole time. Moving around changes your volume.

How to read

  • Read like you’re telling a friend something, not performing it. No funny voices unless the text really needs it.
  • Slower than feels natural. You’ll feel like you’re talking too slowly. You aren’t.
  • Take a proper breath at commas and full stops. You can cut them later if they’re loud.
  • Stumble? Just stop, pause, and re-read the sentence from the start. Don’t start the whole page again. Leave a couple of seconds of silence so the editing is easy.
  • Record in chunks of ten or fifteen minutes. Drink water. ADHD folks especially: breaks are part of the job, not a failure.
  • Say names of the same person the same way every time.

Start and end of each file

At the start say: “[Title], by [Author]. Chapter [number], [chapter title]. Read by [your name or handle].”

At the end say: “End of chapter [number].”

If you’ve got a licence line, say it once at the start of chapter one only: “This recording is released under [licence].”

Editing (optional but worth it)

Free tool: Audacity. You only need to do four things.

  1. Cut out the mistakes and the long silences.
  2. Cut loud breaths if they bother you.
  3. Apply Noise Reduction once if there’s hum in the background. Don’t go heavy or you’ll sound like a robot underwater.
  4. Use Normalize or Loudness Normalization so it’s not much quieter or louder than other chapters.

If you can’t be bothered with editing, upload the rough version and say so. Someone else can tidy it.

Saving the file

  • Keep your raw recording somewhere you control. We won’t have a copy.
  • Export as MP3 (128 kbps or higher, mono is fine) or Opus, or FLAC if you want to be fancy.
  • Name it like this: title_ch01_yourhandle.mp3 (lowercase, underscores, no spaces).

Then

Upload it following the rights and uploading instructions, post the link where you claimed the chapter, and have a lie down. You just made a book free for someone.
